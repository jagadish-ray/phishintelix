Email Security

Spotting Phishing Emails

Urgent language, fake sender domains, and masked links — the social engineering patterns that fool millions every year.

📅 2026⏱ 6 min read🎯 Beginner

A Real-Looking Phishing Email

Study this example carefully — every red flag is annotated:

From:security-alert@paypal-support-center.com ⚠ FAKE DOMAIN
To:you@gmail.com
Subject:⚠️ Your account has been LIMITED — Action Required ⚠ URGENT
Dear Customer, ⚠ GENERIC

We have detected unusual sign-in activity on your PayPal account.
Your account access has been temporarily limited.

You must verify your identity within 24 hours ⚠ PRESSURE
to avoid permanent suspension of your account.⚠ THREAT

→ Click here to verify your account now⚠ HIDDEN LINK
http://paypal-secure-verify.xyz/login

If you do not verify, your account will be closed.

PayPal Security Team

Red Flags Explained

📧
Fake Sender Domain
The email is from paypal-support-center.com, not paypal.com. Always check the full domain after the @ symbol, not just the display name.
👤
Generic Greeting
"Dear Customer" instead of your real name. PayPal and your bank always address you by name in official emails.
⏰
Artificial Urgency
"24 hours", "permanent suspension", "action required" — these phrases are designed to stop you from thinking clearly and make you act fast.
🔗
Mismatched Link
The button says "Click here" but the actual URL goes to paypal-secure-verify.xyz — not PayPal at all. Always hover over links before clicking.
🔤
Suspicious Domain
The .xyz TLD is a major red flag. PayPal only sends emails from @paypal.com — never from random domains.

Common Social Engineering Patterns

Fear & Threats

"Your account will be suspended", "Unauthorized access detected", "Final warning"

Urgency

"Within 24 hours", "Immediately", "Act now", "Last chance", "Expires today"

Rewards

"You have won", "Congratulations", "Claim your prize", "Free gift selected"

Authority

"Your bank", "IRS", "Microsoft Support", "Government notice", "Legal action"

Curiosity

"Someone shared a file with you", "You have a pending delivery", "Your package"

Helpfulness

"We noticed a problem with your account", "We need to verify", "Update your details"

How to Check a Suspicious Email

✅ Check the sender domain — click on the sender name to reveal the full email address. The domain must match exactly (paypal.com, not paypal-secure.com).

✅ Hover over links — before clicking any link, hover your mouse over it. The real destination URL appears in the bottom status bar of your browser.

✅ Copy the link and scan it — paste any suspicious link into PhishIntelix to check it before visiting.

✅ Go directly — if your bank emails you, close the email and type your bank's URL directly into the browser instead of clicking the link.

✅ Check for SPF/DKIM — in Gmail, click the three dots → "Show original" → look for SPF: PASS and DKIM: PASS. Phishing emails often fail these checks.

✅ Golden rule

No legitimate company will ever ask you to verify your password, credit card number, or OTP via email. If you receive such a request, it is almost certainly a phishing attempt.
