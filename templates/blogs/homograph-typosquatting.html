Homograph & Typosquatting Attacks

How look-alike domains fool the human eye — and how machine learning catches what we miss.

📅 2026⏱ 7 min read🎯 Advanced

What Is Typosquatting?

Typosquatting (also called URL hijacking) is when an attacker registers a domain name that is a slight misspelling of a popular brand. Users who mistype a URL, or who don't look carefully at a link, land on the attacker's fake site instead of the real one.

How common is it? Studies show that for every major brand domain, there are dozens of registered typosquatting variants. PayPal alone has hundreds of lookalike domains registered at any given time.

What Is a Homograph Attack?

A homograph attack (also called a homoglyph attack) is far more deceptive. Instead of misspelling a domain, the attacker replaces ASCII characters with visually identical Unicode characters from other alphabets. The domain looks exactly like the real one to the human eye.

Look-alike characters in action:

a

Latin 'a'
U+0061 (REAL)

а

Cyrillic 'а'
U+0430 (FAKE)

o

Latin 'o'
U+006F (REAL)

о

Cyrillic 'о'
U+043E (FAKE)

Can you spot the fake?
Real: https://www.apple.com/account
Fake: https://www.аpple.com/account ← Cyrillic 'а' in position 1
⚠ They look identical in most fonts!

Your browser may show a punycode version like xn--pple-43d.com in the address bar if you look carefully — that's the giveaway. Always check the address bar, not the link text.

How PhishIntelix Detects These

PhishIntelix uses two techniques to catch these attacks automatically:

1. Levenshtein Distance — measures the "edit distance" between a domain name and a list of 25+ popular brands. A distance of 1 or 2 triggers a typosquatting warning.

2. Unicode Normalization — converts the domain to ASCII using NFKD normalization. If the normalized form differs from the original, a homograph attack is flagged.

How to Protect Yourself

✅ Always type important URLs directly into the browser address bar — never click links from emails.

✅ Bookmark your bank, PayPal, and email login pages.

✅ Check the address bar for punycode (xn--) which signals Unicode substitution.

✅ Use PhishIntelix to scan any URL before entering credentials.
