Email Header Analysis — SPF, DKIM & DMARC

By PhishIntelix · Email Authentication & Header Forensics · 10 min read

Every email you receive contains hidden metadata called email headers. These headers tell the full story of where the email came from, which servers it passed through, and whether the sender is who they claim to be. Reading email headers is one of the most powerful ways to detect phishing and spoofed emails.

What Are Email Headers?

Email headers are lines of metadata attached to every email before the visible body content. They are hidden from normal view but contain critical authentication information. In Gmail, you can see them by clicking ⋮ → Show original.

Delivered-To: you@gmail.com
Authentication-Results: mx.google.com;
   dkim=pass header.i=@paypal.com
   spf=pass (google.com: domain of noreply@paypal.com...)
   dmarc=pass (p=REJECT) header.from=paypal.com
From: PayPal <noreply@paypal.com>
Return-Path: <noreply@paypal.com>
Reply-To: <noreply@paypal.com>
Received: from mail.paypal.com (72.33.12.89)

SPF — Sender Policy Framework

SPF answers: Is this mail server authorised to send emails for this domain?

Every domain publishes a list of authorised mail servers in its DNS records. When an email arrives, the receiving server checks if the sending server's IP is on that list.

DNS TXT record for paypal.com:
v=spf1 include:pp.spf.paypal.com -all

# -all = reject all servers NOT on this list
SPF ResultMeaningRisk
passSending server is authorised✅ Safe
failServer NOT authorised — likely spoofed🔴 HIGH
softfailNot fully authorised — suspicious🟡 MEDIUM
noneNo SPF record exists🟡 MEDIUM
neutralDomain made no assertion🟡 MEDIUM
SPF only verifies the sending server — it does NOT verify the message content or the visible From address. This is why DKIM and DMARC are also needed.

DKIM — DomainKeys Identified Mail

DKIM answers: Was this email sent by the claimed domain and has the content been tampered with?

The sending server signs the email with a private cryptographic key. The receiving server verifies the signature using a public key from the domain's DNS. If the signature is valid, the message is authentic and unmodified.

DKIM-Signature: v=1; a=rsa-sha256; d=paypal.com; s=mail;
  h=from:to:subject:date;
  bh=base64_body_hash;
  b=base64_signature_of_headers
DKIM ResultMeaningRisk
passSignature valid — message not tampered✅ Safe
failSignature INVALID — forged or altered🔴 HIGH
noneNo DKIM signature found🟡 MEDIUM
permerrorSignature misconfigured🟡 MEDIUM

DMARC — Domain-based Message Authentication

DMARC builds on SPF and DKIM. It tells receiving servers what to do when an email fails authentication and ensures the visible From address matches the authenticated domain.

DNS TXT record for paypal.com:
v=DMARC1; p=reject; sp=reject;
rua=mailto:dmarc@paypal.com

# p=reject = reject emails that fail DMARC
# p=quarantine = send to spam
# p=none = do nothing (monitoring only)
DMARC ResultMeaningRisk
passPassed domain alignment policy✅ Safe
failFailed alignment — strong phishing indicator🔴 HIGH
noneNo DMARC policy found🟡 MEDIUM

Other Header Red Flags

From vs Return-Path Mismatch

The From header is what you see in your inbox. The Return-Path is where bounced emails go. In legitimate emails these domains match. In phishing emails they often differ.

From: PayPal <security@paypal.com>
Return-Path: <bounce@random-smtp.ru> ← MISMATCH — RED FLAG

Reply-To Hijacking

The Reply-To header tells your email client where to send replies. Attackers set this to their own address so when you reply, your message goes directly to them — even if the From address looks legitimate.

From: support@paypal.com
Reply-To: attacker@harvest-credentials.xyz ← HIJACKED

X-Mailer — Bulk Sender Detection

The X-Mailer header reveals what software was used to send the email. PHPMailer is commonly used by phishing campaigns because it's free and easy to use for mass sending.

X-Mailer: PHPMailer 6.1.8 ← Suspicious — bulk mailer
Important: Even if SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all pass, an email can still be phishing — for example if the attacker has compromised a legitimate account, or if the domain has weak DMARC policy (p=none).

How to Get Headers in Gmail

Open any email in Gmail → click the three dots ⋮ (top right corner of the email) → click Show original → a new tab opens showing the full raw headers.
