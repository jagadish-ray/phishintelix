10 Signs of a Phishing Website

Quick checks you can do before entering any sensitive information on a website.

📅 2026⏱ 5 min read🎯 Beginner

Before you type your password or credit card number into any website, run through this checklist. Phishing sites often have multiple red flags — the more you spot, the more suspicious the site.

1

Suspicious or Misspelled Domain

Check the domain name in the address bar carefully. Attackers use names like paypa1.com, arnazon.com, or apple-id-verify.net to trick you.

⚠ paypa1.com instead of paypal.com
2

No HTTPS or Padlock

Legitimate sites use HTTPS. If the address starts with http:// or your browser shows "Not Secure", do not enter any information.

⚠ http://login.yourbank.com
3

Unusual or Cheap TLD

Phishing sites frequently use cheap or free top-level domains like .xyz, .tk, .top, .cc, or .ml because they are almost free to register.

⚠ secure-bank-login.xyz
4

Excessive Subdomains

Legitimate sites rarely use more than one subdomain. Three or more subdomains is a common trick to make the fake domain appear trustworthy.

⚠ secure.login.paypal.verify.xyz
5

IP Address as Hostname

Real companies never use raw IP addresses as their website address. If you see http://192.168.1.50/login, it is almost certainly malicious.

⚠ http://203.45.12.99/paypal/signin
6

Urgent or Threatening Language

Phishing pages often show alarming messages like "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!" to rush you into acting without thinking.

⚠ "Act immediately or lose access forever!"
7

Poor Grammar and Spelling

Many phishing pages are created quickly or translated poorly. Look for awkward phrasing, grammar mistakes, or inconsistent fonts.

⚠ "Please to verify your informations below."
8

Requests for Unusual Information

A legitimate bank login only needs your username and password. If the site asks for your PIN, mother's maiden name, or full card number upfront, leave immediately.

⚠ Login page asking for SSN + card CVV
9

URL Shortener or Redirect

Links from bit.ly, tinyurl.com or similar services hide the real destination. Always expand shortened URLs before clicking.

⚠ bit.ly/3xKj9z → real URL hidden
10

Mismatched or Missing Favicon

Phishing sites often forget to copy the favicon (the small icon in the browser tab) or use the wrong one. A missing or generic favicon is a warning sign.

⚠ "PayPal" site with a blank or different icon
