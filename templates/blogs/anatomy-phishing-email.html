Anatomy of a Phishing Email

A dissected look at every component attackers craft to make phishing emails convincing — and how ML will detect them.

📅 2026⏱ 7 min read🎯 Intermediate

The Complete Phishing Email — Dissected

Every part of a phishing email is carefully crafted. Below is a real-world-style example with every component colour-coded and explained.

From:"Microsoft Account Team" <noreply@microsofft-security.com>⚠ FAKE DOMAIN
Subject:🔒 Unusual sign-in activity detected on your account⚠ FEAR TRIGGER
To:victim@gmail.com
Dear Microsoft User,⚠ GENERIC GREETING

We detected a sign-in attempt to your Microsoft account from an unrecognised device in Russia (Moscow). If this wasn't you, your account may be compromised.⚠ FEAR + FALSE URGENCY

To secure your account, you must verify your identity within 12 hours or your account will be permanently locked.⚠ TIME PRESSURE + THREAT

→ Verify My Account Now⚠ MASKED LINK → evil-site.xyz

Microsoft Security Team © 2026 Microsoft Corporation One Microsoft Way, Redmond, WA⚠ FAKE LEGITIMACY

Generic Greeting

Real companies address you by your full name. "Dear User" or "Dear Customer" signals a mass phishing blast.

Fear Trigger

False claim of account compromise, suspicious location, or unauthorized access to activate your fight-or-flight response.

Time Pressure + Threat

"12 hours", "permanently locked" — designed to stop rational thinking and force immediate action.

Masked Phishing Link

Button text says "Verify My Account" but the actual href points to a completely different malicious domain.

Fake Legitimacy Footer

Copied company address, copyright, and branding from the real company to appear official.

Social Engineering Keyword Patterns

Machine learning models trained to detect phishing emails scan for clusters of these patterns. The more that appear together, the higher the phishing probability.

Hidden Techniques in HTML Emails

Anchor Text Mismatch

The most common technique — the visible link text says one thing, the actual href goes somewhere else entirely:

Visible: https://microsoft.com/account/verify
Actual: <a href="http://microsofft-phish.xyz/steal">https://microsoft.com/account/verify</a>

Zero-Width Characters

Attackers insert invisible Unicode characters (zero-width spaces, soft hyphens) inside URLs or email addresses to bypass keyword filters while remaining invisible to the human reader.

Image-Only Emails

Some phishing emails contain only a single image (a screenshot of a fake invoice or alert) with no text at all — bypassing text-based spam filters. The entire image is one clickable link.
